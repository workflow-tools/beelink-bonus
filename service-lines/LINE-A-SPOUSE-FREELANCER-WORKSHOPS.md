## 1. Verdict

Do this, but do it once, small, in person, and as an experiment rather than as a revenue line.

The correct first move is a single paid in-person cohort: four weekly evening sessions, eight to twelve adults, delivered off post in a rented room, priced at €450–600 including VAT, sold entirely by personal invitation. Everything else in this space — self-paced courses, online cohorts, licensing, organisational contracts — either fails a German legal test, fails an enrolment test, or requires a track record this cohort is the cheapest way to manufacture.

**Realistic revenue.** A first cohort of eight at €500 gross is €4,000, which is roughly €3,360 net of 19% German VAT, less perhaps €300 for room hire and materials. Call it €3,000 net against 60–80 hours of first-run effort — about €40 per hour, worse than his existing consulting-grade time is worth. At steady state, three cohorts a year with the material already built and 25–30 hours of effort each, the line yields something like €8,000–12,000 net per year. That is a supplementary income, not a business. Anyone modelling more than that is modelling an audience he does not have.

**The honest biggest risk is enrolment, and it is not close.** It is not the law, not the curriculum, and not the hardware. This audience's default alternative is free, better-branded, longer, and attached to a hiring pipeline: Microsoft Software & Systems Academy runs 20 weeks of live virtual instruction with Microsoft Certified Trainers at no cost, IVMF's Onward to Opportunity includes a paid certification exam, and Hiring Our Heroes distributes Google Career Certificates funded by Google. Against that, the median active-duty spouse earns [$35,000](https://ivmf.syracuse.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/IVMF-Military-Spouse-Employment-Landscape-March-2025-v2.pdf) and 20–22% of those in the labour force are unemployed. The people in most pain have the least cash, and the ones with cash have the least pain. Two of the three federal money rails that could have bridged that gap — SpouseWorks (formerly MyCAA) and SkillBridge — are closed to him by written rule, and the third (VET TEC 2.0) is unreachable from a standing start.

The secondary risk, which is cheap to manage but expensive to ignore, is that marketing to this community is legally constrained in ways that eliminate every obvious channel. Mass, group and captive-audience solicitation is prohibited on the installation; so are official garrison bulletins, marquees, newsletters and webpages; so is using FMWR facilities in support of the business; and Soldiers and Federal employees may not act as his agents ([AER 210-70](https://media.defense.gov/2025/Jan/28/2003633273/-1/-1/0/AER210-70.PDF)). Word of mouth and personal invitation are, effectively, the entire legal funnel.

## 2. The audience, and what the location is actually worth

### What is genuinely here

USAG Bavaria's population is approximately 28,000 across Tower Barracks (Grafenwöhr) and Rose Barracks (Vilseck), a figure that counts service members, retirees, civilian employees and family members together ([Military.com base guide](https://www.military.com/base-guide/us-army-garrison-bavaria)). No published breakdown isolates adult family members, so the true addressable pool of spouses is unknown; a working assumption of several thousand is reasonable but [unverified]. The garrison also covers Hohenfels, Garmisch and the training areas, with 7th Army Training Command, 2nd Cavalry Regiment, 41st Field Artillery Brigade and 18th MP Brigade as major tenants ([USAG Bavaria](https://home.army.mil/bavaria/about)).

The national picture of the audience, from DoD's own 2024 Survey of Active Duty Spouses, is a well-educated population under sustained employment stress:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Active-duty spouses (total) | 561,008 |
| In the civilian labour force | 69% |
| Unemployment, DoD-wide / Army | 20% / 22% |
| Bachelor's degree or higher | 50% (32% bachelor's, 18% advanced) |
| Employed spouses whose employer offers remote/telework | 39% |
| Needed a new licence or credential after last PCS | 28% |

Source: [DoD 2024 Survey of Active Duty Spouses infographic](https://download.militaryonesource.mil/12038/MOS/Infographic/2024-active-duty-spouse-employment-and-education.pdf).

Two further numbers set the commercial ceiling. Median active-duty-spouse income is [$35,000, about 42% below civilian comparators](https://ivmf.syracuse.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/IVMF-Military-Spouse-Employment-Landscape-March-2025-v2.pdf), and self-employment among military spouses runs 7.68% against 13.12% for civilian spouses. MFAN's 2025 survey (released June 2026) puts unemployment among job-seeking spouses at 29.9% with 55.9% reporting underemployment, though a March 2026 analysis found DoD's methodology inflates the figure and that BLS-standard methods would yield roughly 14% ([Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/pay-benefits/2026/06/17/has-the-military-been-miscalculating-spouse-unemployment/)). Take the direction, not the decimal.

### Why the location helps — narrowly

The advantage is not market size. It is four specific things.

**Personal invitation is the only legal marketing channel, and he already has standing.** Because AER 210-70 forecloses mass solicitation, official channels and captive audiences, every seller into this community is reduced to word of mouth. That flattens the advantage of a national provider with a marketing budget. A resident with a German company, a local address and four children in the community starts ahead of any outside vendor, and roughly level with nobody.

**Credential appetite is unusually high.** 28% needed a new licence or credential after their last move. This is a population that habitually re-qualifies, and re-qualifying is a purchase decision they are used to making.

**The on-post academic market has thinned to almost nothing.** Central Texas College closed its Europe operation on 31 July 2023 ([CTC Europe](https://ctcd.edu/Locations/Europe/Jobs)), and USAG Bavaria's Education Center now lists only UMGC and Embry-Riddle for face-to-face classes ([USAG Bavaria Education Center](https://home.army.mil/bavaria/index.php/edcenter)). Nobody local delivers hands-on applied-AI instruction in English.

**English delivery is a real differentiator here and only here.** In the wider Oberpfalz commercial market his functional-but-not-fluent German is a liability. Inside the garrison community it is irrelevant, and being an American who can teach in English is the baseline requirement rather than a handicap.

### What the location does not give him

It does not give him money. It does not give him volume — PCS cycles of two to three years mean the audience fully replaces itself, so every year is a cold start on relationships. And it comes pre-poisoned in one specific way: direct-sales and MLM participation is heavy enough in military communities to have earned a community nickname, and an FTC-hosted study of roughly 350 direct-selling companies found approximately 99% of participants lose money after costs ([Military.com](https://www.military.com/spousebuzz/2018/01/30/truth-about-mlm-businesses-and-how-they-hurt-troops.html); [Texas A&M study](https://oaktrust.library.tamu.edu/items/974d81da-e989-4b58-901b-8667e4bb52ff)). "Start a portable business, be your own boss" is burnt territory. It reads as MLM. Positioning must be plain, institutional and artefact-based.

## 5. Curriculum

The classroom design study's four-week arc holds up, but two things change for adults with an income motive. The grade disappears, so the artefact has to carry all the motivation — which means something is deployed and visible from week one, not week four. And the tools change: students must leave with a stack they can reproduce on their own laptop with their own accounts, because a stack they cannot rebuild is worthless the day the course ends.

**Week 1 — Ship something ugly to a real URL.** Scope one problem the participant actually has, or one a person they know would pay to have solved. Accounts in their own name from minute one: GitHub, the AI coding tool, the hosting provider. By the end of session one there is a live URL with their name on it. The pedagogical point is that deployment is the beginning, not the end; the emotional point is that they leave the first evening with proof.

**Week 2 — Make it do the one thing.** Data model, one real end-to-end workflow, sign-in. This is the week where "vibe coding" stops being sufficient and they learn to read what the model wrote, to say what is wrong with it precisely, and to keep a change under control. Version control is taught here as a safety net, not as bureaucracy.

**Week 3 — Make it survive contact.** Real data instead of placeholder data. Error states. Works on a phone. Basic accessibility — real headings, labelled inputs, keyboard navigation — taught as a professional baseline, not as compliance theatre. A backup and restore they have actually performed once.

**Week 4 — Ship it and take the keys.** Custom domain, installable as a local-first PWA, a README a stranger could follow, and a written maintenance plan: what breaks, how they would notice, what they would charge to fix it. The session ends with each participant demonstrating their app to the room and handing over nothing, because they already own everything.

**What they walk away owning.** The repository, under their own account. The deployed application on their own hosting, on their own domain if they bought one. Every account and API key in their own name, paid by them, portable the day they PCS. A written runbook. And a short, honest one-pager on what this skill is and is not worth in the market.

**On the income framing, be blunt with them.** Upwork's Q2 2026 results reported revenue of $191.7m (down 2% year over year), gross services volume of $966.4m (down 4%), active clients down 4% to 763,000, and full-year guidance cut from $760–790m to $730–750m, with the CEO attributing the shortfall to "a faster pace of AI-related automation" among other factors ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/upwork-q2-2026-ai-disruption-guidance-cut); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/earnings/2026/upwork-navigates-cross-currents-as-ai-reshapes-freelance-demand/)). The growth on the same platform is in AI strategy and consulting work, up roughly 50% year over year — fewer, larger, more complex engagements. The honest teachable freelance path is therefore maintenance and integration retainers, not one-off builds: small-business site maintenance in the $200–500/month band and third-party integrations at $1,000–10,000 each [both indicative, SEO-grade sources, unverified]. Teaching people to compete for junior build work would be selling them into the segment being automated first.

## 6. Where the Beelink fits, and where it does not

**Where it does not fit: as the students' model backend.** Adults paying to learn app-building want the tools they will actually use afterwards — Claude Code, Cursor, Lovable, Replit. Teaching them to build against a Qwen behind a portal in Vilseck hands them a stack they cannot reproduce at home, cannot afford to replicate, and cannot put on a CV. The classroom design study's portal architecture existed to solve a K-12 governance problem (students must never touch a model endpoint or an API key). Adults have no such constraint, and the constraint was never a feature.

**Where the privacy story is real but unsellable to individuals.** No commercial training provider was found differentiating on local or private model hosting; all the "your data never leaves" marketing in the wild is aimed at enterprise inference deployment, not at training buyers [negative finding, search not exhaustive]. An individual paying €500 to learn to build an app does not care where inference runs. Do not put it on the landing page.

**Where it genuinely earns its keep.** Three places. First, his own preparation: generating and stress-testing exercises, drafting worked examples, and reviewing participants' code between sessions at zero marginal cost. Second, any exercise involving a participant's genuinely confidential material, where "this stays on a box in Vilseck" is a real answer. Third, and most importantly for the B2B licensing shape later, the inhouse workshop for a German firm whose exercises run against their own contracts and personnel files — the one thing a cloud-based competitor structurally cannot offer.

**The recommended split.** Students build on commercial cloud tools with their own accounts. The Beelink runs his preparation, his review, and any confidential-document exercise. Nothing in the paid consumer offer depends on it.

**One measurement is mandatory before selling a seat.** The documented profile is roughly 5 tok/s for a 70B Q4 model, sequential runs only, memory-bandwidth-bound, with the 7–13B tier at 80–120 tok/s (owner's own benchmarks, `beelink-bonus/CLAUDE.md` and `vilseckki-datafactory-app/CLAUDE.md`). Concurrent-user capacity has never been measured. If any part of a session depends on the Beelink serving 8–12 people simultaneously, load-test it on a 7–13B model first and cap the cohort at whatever the measurement supports with headroom. Never sell a seat count the machine has not been measured at.

## 7. Legal and compliance

### Installation access and commercial solicitation

The garrison Home-Based Business regime is narrower than commonly assumed, and the online carve-out matters. AER 210-70 §18.d states that businesses in private rental quarters are "not governed by this regulation," and that the trigger for approval is commercial solicitation conducted *on* Army installations. The USAG Bavaria HBB packet separately exempts fully online operations: "If you are teleworking for another company or 100% online AND you do not see clients in-person, or have products in hand, no."

So the position depends on facts he must confirm in writing:

- **If he lives in private (non-Government-leased) rental quarters**, the HBB regime does not govern the business itself.
- **If any customer-facing activity happens on the installation**, or if he solicits there, he needs commercial solicitation privileges regardless of where he lives.
- **In-person teaching off post, to community members, with customers found by personal invitation** is the cleanest configuration — and it is the one recommended here.

There is a second regime the obvious reading misses. AER 210-70 Section IV, §16 and §17, governs "online, virtual, hybrid, or blended courses" sold to the military community. Three consequences: approval comes in writing from the IMCOM-Europe ICAO, not from the garrison HBB office; requests must be coordinated with the servicing education services officer, and that decision may not be delegated to them; and §16.a exempts educational institutions approved by USEUCOM to offer programmes on installations — which is why UMGC is outside the regime that would bind him personally. §16.c closes the one on-post noticeboard that would have mattered: "Installation education centers will not be used for soliciting enrollment in online, virtual, hybrid, or blended courses," with catalogues displayable only if the school is approved for VA education benefits, plus a DoD non-endorsement disclaimer.

**Prohibited marketing, verbatim from the regulation and the garrison packet:** soliciting personnel in a "mass," "group," or "captive" audience; unsolicited in-person or telephone contact; emailing DoD personnel on Government computers absent an existing relationship; using an installation daily bulletin, marquee, newsletter, webpage or other official means to announce an agent's presence; bazaar participation without a signed contract and written garrison-commander approval; using FMWR facilities or Government property in support of the business; and employing Soldiers or Federal employees as agents.

**The 14-day cancellation clause is real, but cite it correctly.** It sits at AER 210-70 §17.a(4) and §19(e). §19(f) is an unrelated provision about not modifying Government-owned quarters — quoting it will make a garrison official or a lawyer conclude he has not read the regulation.

**SOFA status is the risk nobody prices.** AER 210-70 §19.c(2): "In most HNs, running a private business or seeking employment on the economy may require a residence permit, despite SOFA exemptions... Loss of or other impact to SOFA status may occur in some countries when attempting to run a private business or seeking local employment." His status derives from his spouse's DoDEA employment. This should be settled with the legal assistance office in writing before, not after.

**Conflict of interest — corrected after cross-document review.** An earlier draft of this section claimed FAR 3.601 "bites on any federal instrument" because of a DoDEA-employed spouse. **That is wrong.** FAR 3.601 runs against the *contracting officer* and prohibits award to a business owned or substantially owned by a **Government employee**. Ryan is not a Government employee, and a spouse's employment does not make her an owner or controller of a UG whose shares he holds. What actually binds is **18 U.S.C. 208** and **5 CFR 2635.502**, and both bind *her* — they restrict her participation in matters affecting his financial interest, not the company's ability to exist or contract. The HBB packet's supervisor-memorandum requirement where a DoD/DA/NAFI employee is involved is real and separate. Practical effect: **federal instruments are not categorically closed**, and the organisational-contract shape deferred in §4 was deferred on a mistaken ground. It should be re-evaluated on its real merits (registration burden, youth-programme scope) rather than treated as legally barred. This would change if Ryan himself became a federal employee — see LINE-B §2.

### German business and VAT treatment

**Keep the teaching inside the UG.** A UG is gewerblich by legal form under §2(2) GewStG, so Gewerbesteuer applies from the first euro of profit with no €24,500 Freibetrag (that allowance is for natural persons and Personengesellschaften only). The obvious workaround — teaching personally as a freelancer to claim freiberufliche "unterrichtende Tätigkeit" treatment — walks into §2 Satz 1 Nr. 1 SGB VI, which makes self-employed "Lehrer und Erzieher" without an insured employee compulsorily insured in the statutory pension scheme. **Correction after review:** an earlier draft framed this as "18.6% of income, worse than the trade tax it avoids." That comparison does not hold — contributions are capped at the Beitragsbemessungsgrenze, they purchase a pension entitlement rather than vanishing, and the comparison omits the corporate stack entirely (roughly 30% at entity level, then Abgeltungsteuer on distribution, on the order of 48% combined on money actually reaching him personally). The honest statement is that **the two routes are not obviously ranked and the analysis needs a Steuerberater, not a heuristic.** The liability-shield point stands on its own. Get the Steuerberater to confirm in writing whether a controlling Gesellschafter-Geschäftsführer of a training UG is himself caught by §2 Nr. 1 — that is the one point where a wrong assumption is recurring and expensive. Vilseck's actual Gewerbesteuer-Hebesatz is [unverified]; get it from the Gemeinde.

**Charge 19% VAT and do not chase the education exemption.** §4 Nr. 21 UStG letter c) covers Schul- und Hochschulunterricht delivered by Privatlehrer — natural persons only, and limited by subject matter to school and university teaching, which a four-week applied course is not. Letter a) requires a Bescheinigung from the competent Landesbehörde and, once granted, destroys the right to Vorsteuerabzug on the Beelink, electricity, software and professional fees. For consumers the exemption would be worth something; for a business that wants its input VAT back, it is a net loss.

**Price accordingly.** Individual participants cannot reclaim VAT, so a €500 sticker price is roughly €420 net to the UG. Model the business on net.

**SOFA-status customers can buy VAT-free.** Under Art. 67(3) of the NATO SOFA Supplementary Agreement, implemented by AE Reg 215-6, the vendor invoices net, completes and stamps the Abwicklungsschein and AE Form 215-6B, returns the original and pink copies to the customer, and retains the yellow copy as proof to the Finanzamt. Above €2,500 a written net offer to CMWRF or the USAFE Services Fund is required first. This is a genuine ~19% price lever on a course sold to a military household, and few German vendors bother with it — but the assessment risk sits with the UG if the forms are wrong, and whether the local tax relief office will issue forms for a *training service* rather than goods is [unverified]. Confirm before advertising it.

**Kleinunternehmerregelung is almost certainly irrelevant.** The §19 UStG thresholds (€25,000 prior year, €100,000 current year) apply to the whole company across all revenue streams, including WritingPAD and the other products. Do not plan around it.

### FernUSG — the constraint that determines format

German distance-learning law is the reason the recommendation is *in person*.

§1 FernUSG requires two elements **cumulatively**: teacher and learner predominantly spatially separated, **and** the teacher or their agent monitoring learning success. BGH III ZR 109/24 (12 June 2025) set the monitoring bar very low — merely being able to ask follow-up questions by chat or email suffices — and extended the statute's protection to entrepreneurs, so a B2B framing buys nothing. Under §7 an unapproved contract is **void**, and the customer can reclaim everything paid.

BGH III ZR 137/25 (5 February 2026) held that predominantly synchronous live-online formats are not distance learning. But it did **not** establish a percentage threshold; the analysis published by the firm reporting the case states explicitly that the decision sets no 50% rule and that the determining factor is whether real-time interaction characterises the offering as a matter of contractual terms. Designing to "51% live" as though it were a safe harbour is exactly the false precision that loses a nullity case.

The practical consequences:

- **In-person delivery is definitively outside FernUSG.** There is no spatial separation. This is the entire reason to accept the cost of a physical room.
- **A recorded, self-paced course sold to German-resident buyers is the worst possible format.** Note that his local audience — US military community members living in Germany — have habitual residence in Germany, so Rome I Art. 6 points German mandatory consumer protection at them. Being American and paying in dollars changes nothing.
- **Geo-gating is a real option for a future asynchronous product.** FernUSG is German law. A recorded course sold to UK, Irish, US and rest-of-world buyers with Germany excluded at checkout carries no FernUSG exposure. Given that his existing revenue is already non-German, this is the correct home for any low-touch product — but it is a different audience from Line A.
- **A second escape route exists and should be put to counsel:** removing learning-success monitoring altogether. Both prongs are required. A course that never assesses, never grades and never gives corrective feedback is arguably outside the statute even if fully asynchronous. A "build your own app with feedback" course almost certainly does monitor learning success, which is why format is load-bearing here.

If any online component is ever sold into Germany, get a written opinion from IT-Recht-Kanzlei (already an existing relationship) covering the contract and the landing page, not just the format. ZFU approval fees are commonly quoted at 150% of the course price with a €1,050 minimum, but the authority's own page states only that fees follow the AVerwGebO NRW [the specific figures are unverified and trace to a commercial content site]. Confirmed from the authority: a three-month processing period and a requirement to submit materials covering the first third of the course at application. §21 FernUSG penalties are tiered — up to €10,000 for the serious offences and up to €1,000 for failure to notify.

### Income claims

**Rule: promise a deliverable, never an income.** No earnings figures, no "replace your salary," no "clients within X weeks," no testimonials that mention money. Promise only what is verifiable — a deployed application the participant owns.

Two independent regimes enforce this. In the US, FTC Section 5 enforcement against coaching sellers is live and expensive: Lurn/Anik Singal surrendered $2.5m with $2.4m refunded to 1,922 consumers; a telemarketing sales-mentor programme drew $16.4m in judgments; in June 2025 the FTC distributed over $2m to 39,500 consumers of coaching schemes. The January 2025 proposals that would have added civil penalties — expanding 16 CFR 437 to cover business coaching and creating a standalone Earnings Claim Rule at 16 CFR 462 — remain proposed, and were approved 3-2 with Commissioners Ferguson and Holyoak dissenting; Ferguson has chaired the Commission since January 2025, so finalisation should be treated as unlikely rather than pending. The Section 5 exposure exists regardless of the rulemaking.

In Germany, §5 UWG makes a misleading success promise actionable even if the contract disclaims it, and Abmahnungen are initiated cheaply by competitors and Verbände rather than only by regulators. Copy must be written to German standards from the first draft. Retrofitting is expensive.

## 8. Go-to-market

The funnel is small, and it is legally prescribed. Everything below is either explicitly permitted or explicitly not prohibited; anything not listed here should be assumed prohibited until the SJA says otherwise.

**Named channels, in the order they should be used.**

1. **Direct personal invitation.** He knows people. His spouse knows people. His children's classmates' parents are the exact demographic. A named individual asked directly is the highest-converting and most clearly lawful approach available. Twelve seats is twelve conversations, not a campaign.
2. **Private community Facebook groups for Grafenwöhr and Vilseck.** Private groups run by community members are not official garrison channels and are not a captive audience. Post as a participant, not as an advertiser, and do not cross-post.
3. **The garrison Home-Based Business Directory**, if he registers. This is the one sanctioned advertising channel on post. Whether anyone reads it is [unverified] and worth checking before treating it as a channel at all.
4. **A free, genuinely non-commercial session with ACS Employment Readiness.** GAO found participation in the spouse scholarship programme was limited mainly by awareness — usage fell from roughly 38,000 spouses in FY2011 to about 21,000 in FY2017, roughly 7% of eligible spouses, with about half of eligible non-users simply unaware the programme existed. A free "how to actually use your $4,000 SpouseWorks benefit" session is real community value and builds standing with ACS and the Education Center. It must be firewalled from the paid offer: the moment it becomes a funnel it risks the mass/group/captive-audience prohibition. Treat it as reputation, not lead generation, and ask the ACS Employment Readiness Program Manager where they draw the line.
5. **One Volkshochschule course** in Amberg-Sulzbach or Weiden as a paid live test of the material. Rates run €20–45 per 45-minute Unterrichtseinheit, so a 20-UE course pays €400–900. That is not revenue; it is a rehearsal room, a local institutional reference and a list of local employers whose staff attended.
6. **IHK Akademie Ostbayern (Weiden, roughly 30 minutes away)** as a Dozent, later. They already sell AI certificate programmes to regional professionals and own the enrolment pipeline, the classroom and the certificate. Delivering as their instructor is far cheaper customer acquisition than building his own cohort — but the rate is [unverified] and this is a different audience from Line A.

**Explicitly do not:** pitch to an assembled group anywhere on post; hand out flyers or cards on the installation; ask a Soldier or DoD civilian to promote the business; email DoD personnel at their work addresses; seek a listing in a garrison newsletter, on the marquee, or on an official webpage; try to put a catalogue in the Education Center; or take a bazaar table without a signed contract and written garrison-commander approval.

**How the first cohort actually fills.** Twelve named people, asked personally, with a specific date, a specific room and a specific price. If twelve conversations do not produce eight deposits, that is the market speaking, and the correct response is to stop — not to buy ads.

## 9. Time budget and the first 90 days

**The honest position: this does not fit 15–25 minutes a day, and pretending otherwise wastes a quarter.**

Building the first cohort's material is 40–60 hours. At 20 minutes a day that is four to six months of elapsed time before a single euro arrives. Delivering four evening sessions is four immovable three-hour blocks plus travel plus preparation. For a man with a full-time job and four children, that is not a marginal commitment.

The way it works, if it works, is that the daily budget funds *maintenance and outreach* while the build happens in a small number of concentrated blocks — two or three weekends, or a week of evenings. If those blocks cannot be found, this line should not be started. That is a scheduling decision to make honestly up front rather than discover in month three.

**Days 1–30 — Verify, do not build.**

Nothing is written until the legal and demand questions are answered, because both can kill the shape. Ask the legal assistance office, in writing, whether operating the business and teaching locally affects SOFA status. Read the lease to establish whether quarters are Government-leased or private, and confirm with ACS whether HBB registration is required for the recommended configuration. Have twelve conversations with named prospects and ask directly what they would pay and what evening works. Ask ACS Employment Readiness what free programmes they already refer spouses to — if MSSA and Onward to Opportunity are already well-known locally, that is decisive information. Get the Vilseck Hebesatz. Send one email to UMGC Europe HR about the adjunct posting. Total: perhaps eight hours across the month, which the daily budget covers.

**Days 31–60 — Build only if the conversations came back warm.**

Warm means at least six of twelve said yes to a specific date and price, and at least three would pay a deposit. If it is fewer, stop here and write down why. If it is enough: build the four-session arc, the exercises, and the participant runbook in concentrated blocks. Book the room. Get the contract drafted with the 14-day cancellation clause. Buy liability insurance — combined IT-Haftpflicht for a small German IT provider runs roughly €145–1,065 per year at €25,000 turnover, and it is a prerequisite for any organisational contract later anyway. Load-test the Beelink if any session depends on it.

**Days 61–90 — Sell, deliver, and measure.**

Collect deposits. Run the cohort. Measure three things and write them down: how many enrolled against how many were asked, how many finished, and how many actually deployed something they still use a month later. Ask every participant, in person, for one sentence about the artefact and nothing about money. Those three numbers decide whether cohort two happens and whether the licensing shape is real.

## 10. What to verify before acting

| Question | Method | Why it gates |
|---|---|---|
| Does operating the business and teaching locally affect SOFA status? | Written query to the garrison legal assistance office, citing AER 210-70 §19.c(2). | The family's legal residence basis. Nothing else matters if this is wrong. |
| Are his quarters Government-leased or private rental? | Read the lease; confirm with ACS. | Determines whether the HBB regime applies at all (§18.d). |
| Does an off-post, in-person, invitation-sold course require garrison approval? | Ask ACS HBB and, if unclear, the garrison SJA. Get it in writing. | Determines whether there is a 3–5 month lead time or none. |
| Would the ACS-hosted free session cross the mass/group/captive-audience line? | Ask the ACS Employment Readiness Program Manager, then the SJA if ambiguous. | The reputation channel is worthless if it is also a violation. |
| Will eight to twelve named people pay €450–600 for four evenings? | Twelve direct conversations. A refundable deposit is the only real signal; a verbal yes is not. | This is the whole business case. |
| What free programmes already reach this community? | Ask ACS what they refer people to; ask three spouses what they have already been offered. | If MSSA and O2O are well-known locally, the paid offer has no wedge. |
| Vilseck's Gewerbesteuer-Hebesatz | Call the Gemeinde. | Sizes the tax cost of routing revenue through the UG. |
| Is a controlling Gesellschafter-Geschäftsführer of a training UG caught by §2 Nr. 1 SGB VI? | Written opinion from the Steuerberater, ideally with a Rentenberater. | A wrong assumption here is recurring and expensive. |
| Will the local tax relief office issue AE Form 215-6B and an Abwicklungsschein for a training service? | One call to the Grafenwöhr tax relief office. | Determines whether the ~19% SOFA price lever exists. |
| FernUSG exposure for any online element | Written opinion from IT-Recht-Kanzlei covering contract and landing page. | §7 nullity means open-ended refund liability, not a fine. |
| Beelink concurrent-user capacity on a 7–13B model | Load test with 8–12 simulated concurrent generations before selling a seat. | Never sell a seat count the machine has not been measured at. |
| Does he hold a completed master's? | He knows. | Binary gate on the UMGC adjunct route. |
| Does the HBB Directory have readers? | Look at it. Ask three community members if they have used it. | If it is a dead PDF, the only sanctioned on-post advertising channel is worthless. |
