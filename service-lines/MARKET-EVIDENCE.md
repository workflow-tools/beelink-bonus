## Verdict

The research programme produced a large volume of accurate primary-source legal and regulatory material and almost no evidence of demand. Four independent verification passes converged on the same three conclusions.

**First, the negative findings are the durable output and they are well-evidenced.** Direct provider status under the DoD spouse scholarship is not achievable on current facts. SkillBridge and MSEP cannot pay him at all. VET TEC 2.0 is unreachable from a standing start. The DoDEA professional-development market open to a new entrant is roughly $12,000 per year globally. Predominantly recorded online courses sold to German buyers are void contracts. Every one of these survived adversarial checking.

**Second, the positive findings are weaker than their confidence labels claimed.** The two flagship opportunities each rest on a factual error large enough to change the recommendation. The EU AI Act Article 4 "buying trigger" does not exist — Article 4 carries no fine under Article 99, market-surveillance powers began 2 August 2025 rather than 2026, and the Digital Omnibus dates cited were not in the cited source. The DoDEA graduate-credit thesis rests on a salary page that does not contain the lane structure, the accreditation rule, or any statement that extension credit qualifies.

**Third, nothing in the dossier uses the Beelink for anything a laptop could not do, and nothing fits 15–25 minutes a day.** Every recommended opportunity is a job, a contract, a paperwork rail, or a live-teaching commitment. The one shape that would fit the time budget — a recorded self-paced course — is precisely the shape German law voids and Anthropic gives away free.

**Recommended path: launch ProPrecept Ireland and ProPrecept RSA.** Both are code-complete, in a market he understands, with no accreditation gate, no garrison approval, no new regulatory surface, and no audience-building cost. That is the benchmark every option in this dossier should have been measured against and none was.

**Runner-up: apply to the UMGC Europe adjunct post in Artificial Intelligence at Grafenwöhr.** It is a real, open, verified vacancy in his exact subject in his own town, hired by a university rather than by the government, so it carries no SAM registration, no FAR 3.601 problem, and no Privacy Act exposure. It is not passive and it does not use the hardware. Treat it as a credential purchase, and confirm two things first: that he holds a completed master's, and that taking local employment does not affect the SOFA status his family lives under.

## 1. How to read this file

Confidence markers follow a fixed scheme. **Verified** means the exact text or figure was retrieved from the primary source named. **Reported** means a credible secondary source states it and the primary was not retrieved. **[unverified]** means no source was located, or the cited source does not contain the claim. Where a research pass asserted something and a verification pass checked it against the primary document, **the verifier wins without exception** — refuted claims are recorded in Section 7 for traceability and are never propagated as fact elsewhere in this file or in the service-line documents. Several claims below carry a lower confidence here than they did in the original research; that downgrade is deliberate and load-bearing. Where a figure has a plausible-looking precision that its source does not support (four significant figures from a rounded secondary report, a dollar cap absent from the cited PDF), it is marked unverified even though the direction of the claim may be right.

### 2.3 AER 210-70 — the regulation that actually governs selling into this community

This is the most-misread document in the dossier. Corrected positions:

- **Home-Based Business approval attaches to Government-owned or -leased quarters.** §18.d: individuals operating in private rental quarters "must comply with the terms of their lease and HN laws"; such operation "is not governed by this regulation." The installation trigger is commercial *solicitation* conducted on Army installations, not the existence of a business.
- **The garrison's own 2025 HBB packet exempts online-only businesses.** Verbatim: "Teleworking? Please read. If you are teleworking for another company or 100% online AND you do not see clients in-person, or have products in hand, no." An online evening cohort delivered from private rental quarters requires German tax compliance, not HBB approval. **The "3–5 month lead time before the first legal euro" does not apply to that product.**
- **The 14-day cancellation clause is at §17.a(4) and §19(e), not §19(f).** §19(f) is the signed statement that no modifications will be made to Government-owned quarters. The substantive requirement is real; the citation matters because a lawyer or garrison official checking §19(f) finds an unrelated provision.
- **Section IV governs exactly the product the research recommended, and no research pass found it.** §16 "Online, Virtual, Hybrid, or Blended Courses" and §17 "Application Procedures for Vendors": approval comes in writing from the IMCOM-Europe ICAO, not the garrison HBB office — "A vendor may not engage in commercial solicitation until granted written approval to do so from the IMCOM-Europe ICAO." Requests must be coordinated with the servicing education services officer, and the grant/deny decision "may not be delegated to the education services officer."
- **§16.a exempts USEUCOM-approved institutions** offering programmes of study on military installations within USEUCOM. UMGC is outside the vendor regime that would bind an individual. This is the strongest regulatory argument for routing through UMGC.
- **§16.c closes the obvious on-post channel.** "Installation education centers will not be used for soliciting enrollment in online, virtual, hybrid, or blended courses." Catalogues may be displayed only where the school is approved for VA education benefits, with a DoD non-endorsement disclaimer.
- **Prohibited solicitation, verbatim list:** soliciting personnel in a "mass," "group," or "captive" audience; unsolicited in-person or telephone contact; emailing DoD personnel on Government computers absent an existing relationship; using an installation daily bulletin, marquee, newsletter, webpage or other official means to announce an agent's presence; AAFES/FMWR/private-organisation bazaars without a signed contract and written garrison-commander approval; using FMWR facilities or Government property in support of the business; employing Soldiers or Federal employees as agents.
- **SOFA warning, §19.c(2), verbatim:** "In most HNs, running a private business or seeking employment on the economy may require a residence permit, despite SOFA exemptions... Loss of or other impact to SOFA status may occur in some countries when attempting to run a private business or seeking local employment. Members are highly encouraged to consult the legal assistance office." Ryan's status derives from his spouse's DoDEA employment. Taking a UMGC adjunct post is "seeking local employment" on its face, and the posting's "applicant maintains own legal status" language is the institution disclaiming that risk.
- **German tax warning in the garrison fact sheet:** host-nation authorities may levy back taxes on revenues up to seven years into the past plus administrative fines, will rigorously estimate sales where records are inadequate, and can consider worldwide income including non-taxable living allowances and SOFA savings advantages. HBB operators are barred from using military legal or tax assistance for the business.

## 3. DoD acquisition mechanics

### 3.1 Thresholds and authorities

| Item | Value | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Micro-purchase threshold | $15,000 (raised from $10,000 on 1 Oct 2025) | FAR 2.101 |
| Simplified acquisition threshold | $350,000 (from $250,000) | FAR 2.101 |
| Construction carve-out (Davis-Bacon) | $2,000 | FAR 2.101 |
| Service Contract Labor Standards carve-out | $2,500 | FAR 2.101 |
| Purchase card | "the preferred method to purchase and to pay for micro-purchases" | FAR Subpart 13.2 |
| Commercial training via purchase card | "Use the purchase card as a **method of payment** for all commercial training $25,000 and below" | AFARS 5113.270-90 |

**The $25,000 training figure adds nothing over $15,000.** AFARS designates the card as a method of *payment*, not a purchase authority; AFARS 8-1 cross-references FAR Part 13 and carries no dollar thresholds of its own. Above $15,000 a purchase order and FAR 13.104 competition still apply. "No contract, no solicitation, no proposal" is true only below the micro-purchase threshold, where the $25,000 rule is irrelevant.

**SCLS geography works in his favour and is stronger than first stated.** FAR 22.1003-2: "The Service Contract Labor Standards statute does not apply to contracts performed outside the United States." FAR 22.1001 defines "United States" to exclude "any U.S. base or possession within a foreign country." Delivery at Grafenwöhr or Vilseck is therefore outside SCLS and the full $15,000 micro-purchase threshold is available — a fourfold increase over the $2,500 carve-out. (The separate "bona fide professional employees" exemption asserted in research is **not** in FAR Subpart 22.10; 22.1003-3 lists the statutory exemptions and professional status is not among them.)

### 3.2 The small-business set-aside — the barrier nobody checked

**FAR 19.502-2:** each acquisition "above the micro-purchase threshold, but not over the simplified acquisition threshold, shall be set aside for small business" absent a rule-of-two determination. **13 CFR 121.105** requires a small business concern to be "a business entity organized for profit, with a place of business located in the United States."

A German UG with no US place of business cannot qualify. **The entire $15,000–$350,000 band is structurally closed to the UG**, independent of buying habits. This is the mechanism that actually excludes him from education-services awards — not nationality per se, which is why German firms hold large DoDEA primes (custodial, maintenance, student transport) that sit above the SAT or are competed differently.

### 3.3 Conflict-of-interest statutes

**FAR 3.601(a):** "a contracting officer shall not knowingly award a contract to a Government employee or to a business concern or other organization owned or substantially owned or controlled by one or more Government employees." Note the prohibition runs against the contracting officer, not the company, and there is no numeric ownership threshold — the test is "substantially owned or controlled."

**FAR 3.602:** an exception requires "a most compelling reason, such as when the Government's needs cannot reasonably be otherwise met," authorised at agency-head level. Nobody grants that for teacher training a dozen US vendors already sell.

**Today, with Ryan not a federal employee, FAR 3.601 does not bar the UG.** His spouse's DoDEA employment does not make her an owner or controller, and Germany's default marital regime (Zugewinngemeinschaft) keeps spousal assets separate during marriage. The legal burden falls on her instead:

- **18 U.S.C. 208** imputes a spouse's financial interest to the employee.
- **5 CFR 2635.502(b)(1)** gives her a covered relationship twice over — member of household, and a person for whom her spouse serves as officer or contractor. She may not participate in any particular matter involving the UG without an agency-designee determination or written authorisation.

The practical scope is understated in the original research. In a small overseas district where one professional-learning specialist may serve multiple schools, and where the community-superintendent layer was eliminated in April 2026 (collapsing decision-making upward), the probability that she sits somewhere in a decision chain is not low. "Never sell to her school or district" may be insufficient; the safe rule is not to sell to DoWEA Europe at all.

**If Ryan becomes a DoDEA employee, FAR 3.601 bars award outright.** The DoDEA channel is open only while he is not employed there. The two paths are mutually exclusive and should be sequenced deliberately.

### 3.4 Privacy Act, not FERPA

**DoDEA student records are governed by the Privacy Act (5 U.S.C. 552a)** under SORN "DoDEA 26 — Department of Defense Education Activity Educational Records" (76 FR 24001, 29 April 2011; original 72 FR 24572). Stated authorities: 10 U.S.C. 113; 10 U.S.C. 2164; 20 U.S.C. 921–932; 29 U.S.C. 794; DoD Directive 1342.20 and 1020.1; E.O. 9397. **No FERPA, no Department of Education authority anywhere.**

The consequence is stricter, not looser, for a vendor. There is no FERPA-style "school official" exception for contractors. Instead **FAR 24.102** deems a contractor and its employees "employees of the agency for purposes of the criminal penalties of the Act" where the contract provides for **operation** of a system of records; **FAR 24.104** makes clauses 52.224-1 and 52.224-2 mandatory. Design and development trigger the clauses but not the criminal-penalty deeming — the correct scope is narrower than "design, development or operation," and merely designing something does not by itself create personal criminal exposure.

Operationally this is neutral rather than lighter: DoDEA school-level practice and vendor agreements routinely apply FERPA-equivalent protections as policy. A vendor gets a differently-labelled compliance load, not a smaller one, plus the DoDIN Approved Products List / DoD Instruction 8100.04 regime.

**Absolute design constraint that follows:** adult learners only, zero student PII, never operate anything that retrieves records by student name or identifier, nothing hosted on DoDEA's behalf.

### 3.5 Section 508

Federal guidance is explicit that ICT bought through micro-purchase must still meet Section 508 unless a documented exception applies — the pre-2003 micro-purchase exception is gone. DoDEA enforces this at the cardholder stage: a Section 508 micro-purchase training certificate is a prerequisite for GPC cardholder appointment.

Product-shape consequence: a person-delivered workshop plus accessible slides and tagged PDFs is a **service** and clears easily. A Beelink-backed student portal is **ICT** and needs conformance evidence. The 508 wall does not disappear by staying under $15,000.

### 3.6 Registration

SAM.gov entity registration with a UEI is mandatory for any federal award and is free. A non-US entity historically needs an NCAGE code from NATO's NSPA before SAM registration (3–10 business days for NCAGE, 2–4 weeks end to end). A 2026 change removes the NCAGE prerequisite for some international entities not seeking DoD financial assistance, but DoD still validates CAGE/NCAGE prior to award under FAR 4.1802 and DFARS Subpart 204.18. Registration is a zero-cost chore, not a blocker — and should not be done speculatively, only once a named buyer has said they intend to pay.

### 4.2 Marketplace economics

Maven's instructor page states **"$10,000 Average Year 1 earnings."** Maven takes 10% plus Stripe fees; its Student Growth Program can layer discounts up to 30%, affiliate fees up to 30% and B2B partner fees up to 40% on affected sales. Maven's own documentation declines to quantify marketplace-sourced enrolment, saying only that its analysis "looked only at visitors from the Maven marketplace (ie. not direct landing page visits, coming directly from instructor audience)."

**The widely-repeated "80% of sales come from the instructor's own audience" is a 2023 founder tweet, not a statistic.** The direction is credible; the split is not a fact. Either way, an instructor with no audience in the niche has no mechanism to fill a cohort, and audience-building does not fit 15–25 minutes a day. This is the single largest unpriced cost in every consumer-course model in the dossier.

### 4.7 Income-claim regulation

**FTC, 13 January 2025:** NPRM expanding the scope of the Business Opportunity Rule (16 CFR 437) to cover business coaching and money-making opportunities, plus a proposed standalone Earnings Claim Rule (16 CFR 462). Both remain proposed as of August 2026 and appear on the FTC's regulatory agenda.

**The decisive fact omitted from the original research: the NPRM passed 3–2, with Commissioners Ferguson and Holyoak dissenting — and Ferguson has chaired the FTC since January 2025.** A proposed rule the sitting chairman voted against is not on a path to finalisation. Treat finalisation as unlikely.

**Section 5 liability bites regardless and does not depend on the rulemaking.** Lurn/Anik Singal surrendered $2.5m with $2.4m refunded to 1,922 consumers; a telemarketing sales-mentor programme drew $16.4m in judgments on $29m of consumer payments; in June 2024 the FTC sent $2.4m to consumers harmed by a deceptive business-coaching scheme, and in June 2025 paid $2m+ to 39,500 consumers of coaching schemes.

**German layer, independently enforceable.** §5 UWG prohibits misleading commercial practices; practitioner guidance is that a success guarantee on a sales page is abmahnfähig even where the contract disclaims it, and Abmahnungen are brought by competitors and Verbände, not only regulators — so they fire far more often than FTC action.

**Design rule: promise a verifiable artefact ("you will ship a working app"), never an income.** Free at design time, expensive to retrofit.

### 4.8 EU AI Act Article 4 — the corrected position

This is the most consequential correction in the dossier.

| Claim as researched | Corrected position |
|---|---|
| Article 4 breach carries a penalty tier of up to €7.5m or 1% of global turnover | **Article 4 appears in no tier of Article 99.** The three tiers are €35m/7% (Art. 5 prohibited practices), €15m/3% (Arts. 16, 22, 23, 24, 26, 31, 33, 34, 50) and €7.5m/1% (supplying incorrect, incomplete or misleading information to notified bodies or national competent authorities). **There is no direct administrative fine for failing the AI-literacy duty.** |
| National market surveillance authorities gained supervisory powers on 2 Aug 2026 | Penalties and designation of national competent/market-surveillance authorities took effect **2 August 2025**. 2 August 2026 is the date for Art. 50 transparency and (originally) Chapter III high-risk obligations. |
| Article 4 enforcement window opened 2 Aug 2026 | **Article 4 has applied since 2 February 2025** — eighteen months ago. Firms that intended to discharge it have already bought the TÜV course, the IHK seminar or the €14.90 e-learning. |
| Digital Omnibus: Council green light 29 June 2026, act signed 8 July 2026 | Neither date appears in the cited source. Gibson Dunn records a **provisional political agreement on 6 May 2026** with formal adoption and OJ publication expected. | Deployers face civil liability from 2 Aug 2025 for harm caused by inadequately trained staff | **No such regime exists.** The AI Liability Directive was withdrawn by the Commission. Liability rests on ordinary national tort and contract law, unchanged by the AI Act. |

Downstream dates that **do** check out: Annex III high-risk pushed to 2 December 2027; systems embedded in regulated products to 2 August 2028. The Commission confirmed (May 2025) that no formal certification is required for AI literacy. The Omnibus softens Art. 4 from ensuring literacy to supporting its development — an obligation of effort.

**Commercial consequence:** the "statutory, dated, non-optional buying trigger" does not exist. What remains is a stale, unfineable obligation of effort whose standard is about to be softened, with a commodity price floor and free publicly-funded alternatives in the same region.

## 6. German business, tax and training law

### 6.1 FernUSG — the distance-learning statute

**§1 requires TWO conditions cumulatively:** (a) räumliche Trennung — teacher and learner "ausschließlich oder überwiegend räumlich getrennt"; and (b) Überwachung des Lernerfolgs — the teacher or an agent monitors learning success. Both must be present. **In-person delivery is outside the statute entirely**, with or without any case law.

**Case law:**

| Case | Date | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| BGH III ZR 109/24 | 12 June 2025 | Online coaching is Fernunterricht; the Lernerfolgskontrolle threshold is very low — participants being able to ask follow-up questions by chat or email suffices, and "eine aktive Überprüfung durch die Lehrenden ist nicht erforderlich." Protection extends to **Unternehmer/Selbständige** (§14 BGB), not only consumers. |
| LG München I, Az. 47 O 12802/24 | 8 Aug 2025 | More lenient: intensive live interaction and individual support can negate spatial separation even where some asynchronous material exists. |
| BGH III ZR 137/25 | 5 Feb 2026 | Predominantly synchronous live-online formats are **not** distance learning; teleological reduction of räumliche Trennung where teacher and learner can communicate in real time. |
| BGH III ZR 173/24 | Oct 2025 (claimed) | **[unverified]** — no corroboration found; the specialist source cited for it discusses only III ZR 109/24 and the LG München I case. |

**The "≥50% real-time bidirectional communication" safe harbour does not exist.** The cited analysis states explicitly that the decision "does not establish a specific percentage threshold like 50%. The determining factor is whether real-time interaction characterizes the offering, based on contractual terms rather than actual delivery methods." Designing to a stopwatch ratio as if it were a holding is exactly the false precision that loses a nullity case; the test is the concrete contractual design.

**Consequences of being captured without ZFU approval:** the contract is void under **§7 FernUSG** and the customer reclaims every euro under **§812 BGB**, with the BGH setting a near-impossible bar for offsetting value received. There is no time limit tied to the sale. A B2B framing buys no protection.

**§12** exempts courses serving "ausschließlich der Freizeitgestaltung oder der Unterhaltung" from approval — but distribution must still be notified to the authority.

**§5** gives participants a statutory termination right: first possible at the end of the first half-year with six weeks' notice, thereafter at any time with three months' notice, with payment owed only proportionate to services rendered. Written form required. Long lock-in pricing is therefore pointless.

**§21 fines are tiered:** up to €10,000 for Abs. 1 Nr. 1, 3 and 4; **up to €1,000 for Nr. 2 and 5** — and Nr. 2 is the failure-to-notify offence attaching to the §12 leisure-course route.

**Two escape routes, only one of which was analysed.** The first is synchronicity (per III ZR 137/25). The second, cheaper and never considered, is **removing Lernerfolgskontrolle entirely** — a pure sale of video materials with no assessment, no feedback channel, no Q&A and no completion tracking is arguably not Fernunterricht even when fully asynchronous. Given the low bar III ZR 109/24 set, this needs counsel. **A third route was also never considered: geo-gating.** FernUSG is German law; under Rome I Art. 6 a consumer contract is governed by the law of the consumer's habitual residence. Existing revenue (WritingPAD UK, ProPrecept Ireland/RSA) is entirely non-German. A recorded course sold to UK/US/RoW buyers with Germany excluded at checkout carries no FernUSG exposure.

### 6.2 ZFU approval

Verified from the authority's own page: fees follow the **Allgemeine Verwaltungsgebührenordnung NRW (AVerwGebO NRW)**, with the schedule published only as a downloadable PDF table; a three-month processing period applies; and "Es müssen Lernmaterialien für die Module zum ersten Drittel des gesamten Lehrgangs vorgelegt werden" — materials for the first third of the course must exist at filing, with a binding schedule for the rest.

### 6.3 Legal form, trade tax and pension insurance

**A UG can never earn freiberufliche Einkünfte.** Under §2 Abs. 2 GewStG a Kapitalgesellschaft is a Gewerbebetrieb by legal form regardless of activity, so a teaching activity that would be freiberuflich under §18 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 EStG for a natural person is gewerblich inside the UG. **The €24,500 Gewerbesteuer-Freibetrag is unavailable** — it applies only to natural persons and Personengesellschaften. Cost is 3.5% × municipal Hebesatz; at a typical Bavarian Hebesatz around 350% that is ~12% of profit. **Vilseck's actual Hebesatz is [unverified]** — obtain it from the Gemeinde.

**The freelance workaround has a trap.** §2 Satz 1 Nr. 1 SGB VI makes self-employed "Lehrer und Erzieher, die im Zusammenhang mit ihrer selbständigen Tätigkeit regelmäßig keinen versicherungspflichtigen Arbeitnehmer beschäftigen" subject to mandatory statutory pension insurance at 18.6%. Exemptions: §6 Abs. 1a Existenzgründer relief (time-limited); the Geringfügigkeitsgrenze (cited at €603/month for 2026, **[unverified]**).

**The "18.6% is worse than 12%" comparison does not hold as stated.** Three defects: pension contributions are capped at the Beitragsbemessungsgrenze and purchase an entitlement rather than disappearing; the comparison omits the corporate stack (Körperschaftsteuer 15% + Soli + Gewerbesteuer ~12% ≈ 30% at entity level, then ~26.4% Abgeltungsteuer on distribution, ≈48% combined on money reaching him personally) against a single personal-rate charge on freelance income; and which route wins depends almost entirely on whether profits are retained or distributed, a variable never introduced. **This needs a Steuerberater, not a heuristic.**

**Herrenberg and the §127 SGB IV shield.** BSG B 12 R 3/20 R (28 June 2022) held a music-school teacher on a standard Honorarvertrag to be in dependent employment regardless of contract labelling. The §127 SGB IV transitional shield for Honorarlehrkräfte **expires 31 December 2026** — both sources cited for a 2027 extension say 2026 verbatim: "tritt bis zum 31. Dezember 2026 keine Versicherungs- und Beitragspflicht aufgrund einer Beschäftigung ein" and "Eine Versicherungspflicht tritt damit erst am 1. Januar 2027 ein." **No March 2026 extension is evidenced.** Runway for German institutional teaching (VHS, IHK) is roughly four and a half months from today, not sixteen.

### 6.4 VAT

**§4 Nr. 21 UStG** was rewritten by the Jahressteuergesetz 2024 with effect from 1 January 2025.

- **Letter a)** requires a Bescheinigung from the competent Landesbehörde certifying delivery of "Schulunterricht, Hochschulunterricht, Ausbildung, Fortbildung oder berufliche Umschulung."
- **Letter b)** covers "Unterrichtsleistungen selbständiger Lehrer" at Hochschulen or at schools meeting letter a)'s conditions.
- **Letter c)** covers "Schul- und Hochschulunterricht, der von Privatlehrern erteilt wird."

The commonly-repeated framing that letter b) covers Privatlehrer and is barred to a UG because "Privatlehrer" means natural persons only is **wrong on the letter assignment and fixates on the second-order obstacle**. The controlling limit on letter c) is **subject matter**: it reaches only Schul- und Hochschulunterricht, so a corporate AI workshop falls outside it even when delivered by a natural person (ECJ *Haderer* C-445/05; *Eulitz* C-473/08).

**"Freizeitgestaltung" does not appear anywhere in §4 Nr. 21.** The leisure-course exclusion derives from ECJ case law and BMF guidance, making the tension between the §12 FernUSG leisure route and the VAT exemption an administrative-practice matter rather than a statutory one — softer and more arguable than presented.

BMF implementing letters dated **24 October 2025** (III C 2 – S 7179/00054/001/084) allow prior practice to stand for transactions before 1 January 2028.

**Do not chase the exemption.** B2B buyers reclaim 19% so it costs them nothing, while exemption destroys the right to Vorsteuerabzug on hardware, electricity, software and professional fees.

**Kleinunternehmerregelung 2026 (§19 UStG):** €25,000 prior-year and €100,000 current-year thresholds, both must hold. Since 1 January 2025 Kleinunternehmer turnover is echt steuerfrei; new founders start automatically as Kleinunternehmer with no forecast; an EU-wide cross-border small-business scheme now exists. Crossing €100,000 mid-year ends the status immediately and the breaching transaction is itself taxable. Status attaches to the **whole UG across all revenue streams** — WritingPAD/ProPrecept/FacultyWizard revenue counts.

**Place of supply.** Training delivered in Germany is taxed where the event takes place; electronically supplied services to a private customer are taxed at the customer's residence. **A SOFA member living in Germany is resident in Germany for VAT.** US citizenship or USD payment changes nothing. A self-paced online course sold to consumers across multiple EU states means German VAT at 19% domestically plus **OSS registration and quarterly filings** — a permanent overhead never costed in any revenue model in the dossier. A €500 course price is a ~€420 net price.

**US Forces Tax Relief Program** — the only mechanism that removes German VAT on a sale to a SOFA household. Art. 67(3) of the Supplementary Agreement to the NATO SOFA, implemented by Army in Europe Regulation 215-6. The vendor invoices net, completes and stamps the Abwicklungsschein plus AE Form 215-6B, returns original and pink copies to the customer, retains the yellow copy and Abwicklungsschein as proof to the German tax authorities. **Above €2,500 the vendor must first issue a written net offer addressed to CMWRF or the USAFE Services Fund**, or the invoice cannot be accepted. Whether the local tax relief office will issue forms for training or subscription services is **[unverified]**. Risk of incorrect paperwork sits with the vendor: the Finanzamt assesses the VAT against the UG.

### 6.5 Provider accreditation for public funding

**AZAV** (gateway to Bildungsgutschein and §82 SGB III employer-funded training):

### 6.7 Insurance

Combined IT liability (Betriebshaftpflicht + Vermögensschadenhaftpflicht) for a German IT service provider at €25,000 annual turnover: quoted **€12.02–€88.53 per month, roughly €145–€1,065 per year**, with cheaper tariffs for freelancers and small firms up to €100,000 turnover and start-up tariffs up to €250,000. Trainer/Dozent liability is sold separately and many clients expect freelance trainers to carry their own.

The **Vermögensschaden** module — pure financial loss with no accompanying property or personal injury — is the one that matters for software and AI output errors. **No source was found either confirming or denying AI/KI-specific exclusions [unverified].** Get the insurer to confirm in writing that losses from erroneous output of an AI system the UG operates or configures fall inside the cover, and never give a client a written accuracy guarantee — that converts an insured negligence claim into an uninsured warranty claim.

### 6.8 GDPR and professional secrecy

The **Art. 30(5)** exemption for organisations under 250 employees is narrower than usually assumed: it falls away where processing is not occasional, which a continuously running service is not — so a Verzeichnis von Verarbeitungstätigkeiten is required. The Commission's May 2025 Omnibus proposal would raise the threshold to 750 employees and condition it on Art. 35 high-risk; **whether that was adopted is [unverified]**.

Art. 32 requires documented technical and organisational measures. A DPIA under Art. 35 is not triggered by running an LLM as such but is plausibly triggered by large-scale processing of confidential client documents.

**Operational obligations omitted from all research and likely to appear in any client's AVV template:** Art. 33 breach notification within 72 hours (hard to guarantee at 20 minutes a day); Art. 28(3)(h) client audit rights; Art. 32(1)(c) restore-availability testing; deletion or return of data at contract end.

**Professional secrecy.** Serving a Notarin engages **§203 StGB** — mitwirkende Personen must be formally obligated to secrecy. The provision is probably **Abs. 4**, inserted by the 2017 reform on third-party involvement in professional practice, rather than Abs. 3 as commonly cited. Above the criminal-secrecy question sits **notarial supervision**: Notare are public office holders subject to Dienstaufsicht via the Landgericht and their Notarkammer, with their own rules on IT, outsourcing and record custody (BNotO, DONot). Whether a Notarin may route Urkunden through an external provider's LLM at all is a supervision question that must be answered before the criminal-secrecy question matters.

### 6.9 Infrastructure and residential connectivity

Vodafone's Kabel AGB explicitly forbid using the internet access to operate a server and state that private flat rates must not be used commercially, with commercial use rebilled at business-customer rates. Telekom in practice tolerates small, low-traffic private servers. Business lines deliver symmetric bandwidth, static IPs and a contractual Entstörung SLA; private lines rotate IPs (typically every 24h) with no availability guarantee. Business connections start under €100/month.

Two distinct exposures. The AGB exposure is manageable by never exposing inbound ports — Tailscale or Cloudflare Tunnel are outbound-only. **The availability exposure is not fixable by tunnelling**: a paid service with an SLA on a consumer line with no Entstörung guarantee is a contract that cannot be honoured.

**Single point of failure, never addressed in any research pass.** One consumer mini-PC, on residential power and internet, no redundancy, no spare, no documented backup or restore path, operated by someone with 15–25 minutes a day and a documented history of relocation. A dead PSU is a client-facing outage with no failover and no time budget to fix it. This is a stronger practical argument against every recurring-service model in the dossier than anything in the risk sections, and a stronger argument for cloud inference than any cost comparison.

### 6.10 German public-sector cloud posture

The Datenschutzkonferenz concluded in November 2022 that Microsoft 365 was not demonstrably GDPR-compliant; several state DPAs advised against its use in schools. School data protection remains a state-by-state matter with the school as controller. German state schools have no budget and maximal procedural friction; the sovereignty pitch, if it works anywhere, works on fee-paying private and international schools and on private-sector clients.
